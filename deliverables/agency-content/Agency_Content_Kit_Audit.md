# Agency Content Kit Audit

**Decision:** useful framework; approved only with the controls in the production kit.  
**Prepared for:** Hossain Consulting  
**Audit date:** 19 September 2026

## Strong elements retained

- Client context before prompting
- Three drafts followed by human editing
- Real-job input instead of generic volume
- Rejection memory
- Funnel chaining from hook to conversion
- Sustainable five-day rhythm
- Honest acknowledgement that content alone does not distribute or measure itself
- Separation from the recruiter-facing Salesforce portfolio

## Material risks corrected

| Risk in source kit | Correction |
|---|---|
| Precise sample facts in the hook swipe file could be mistaken for reusable claims | Replaced with formula-only hooks; every fact requires a claim ID |
| “Ask instead of inventing” depended on model obedience | Added claims register, workflow states and mandatory human approval |
| Emergency/DIY prompts could create unsafe instructions | Added qualified-review gate and refusal boundaries |
| Regulation/standard prompt could produce obsolete or misapplied law | Requires authoritative current source, jurisdiction, review date and qualified reviewer |
| Email sequences lacked an operational consent/unsubscribe gate | Added consent evidence, sender identity, unsubscribe and suppression controls |
| Lead magnets collected personal information without a defined privacy process | Added collection notice, purpose, access, retention and provider assessment |
| Testimonial prompt mentioned permission but not incentives or evidence retention | Added genuine-review, incentive disclosure and permission records |
| Pricing/guarantee/urgency prompts could mislead | Added scope, GST, evidence, material-condition and capacity controls |
| Local hooks invited unsupported suburb generalisations | Require local sources or clearly limited client observations |
| `rejected.md` could accumulate sensitive client/customer data | Added minimisation and sanitised preference template |
| Automated agent build could imply automatic publishing | Agent is draft-only; distribution requires explicit approvals |

## Examples removed from production use

The original swipe examples involving exact water use, temperatures, structural timelines, soil movement, equipment lifespan, servicing intervals, repair prices and universal housing defects are not approved claims. Some may be defensible in a tightly defined context, but none should be published without current, relevant evidence and qualified review.

## Recommended product packaging

Do not sell “viral content” or guaranteed lead volume. Sell a controlled content and conversion system:

1. **Foundation:** profile/GBP review, claim inventory, content context and measurement setup.
2. **Conversion:** quote follow-up, service page, proposal/call materials and compliant review request.
3. **Content:** real-job educational posts and local discovery content.
4. **Lead capture:** approved lead magnet, privacy/consent flow and nurture sequence.
5. **Optimisation:** monthly evidence-based report and test plan.

The first client pilot should remain manual for 30 days. Automate drafting and workflow only after the approval, consent, publication and measurement records work reliably.

## Authoritative basis checked

- ACCC guidance requires advertising claims to be truthful, accurate, based on reasonable grounds and capable of proof; this extends to social media, websites and testimonials.
- ACMA guidance requires consent before commercial electronic marketing, accurate sender identification/contact information and an unsubscribe facility; unsubscribe requests must generally be honoured within five working days.
- OAIC APP guidance covers collection notices, use/disclosure, direct marketing, security, access/correction and cross-border handling for covered entities. Applicability requires assessment rather than assumption.

This audit is operational guidance and not legal advice.
